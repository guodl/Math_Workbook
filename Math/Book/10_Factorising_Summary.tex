\begin{boxSkill}{Skill   \thenskill \quad Factorising $x^2+mx+n$}
   \pdfbookmark[subsubsection]{Skill \thenskill \quad Factorising}{sknFac01}
  To factorise $x^2+mx+n$:\\
  \-\hspace{1cm}Find two numbers that multiply to give $n$ and also add up to give $m$.
\tcbline 
  For example:\\
  \-\hspace{1cm}Factorise $x^2+11x+24$\\[15pt]
  \-\hspace{3cm} $24 = 3 \times 8$\\
  \-\hspace{3cm} $11 = 3 + 8$ \\[15pt]
  \-\hspace{2cm} $x^2+11x+24 = (x+3)(x+8)$
\end{boxSkill}

\begin{boxSkill}{Skill   \thenskill \quad Factorising by Grouping}
   \pdfbookmark[subsubsection]{Skill \thenskill \quad Factorising by Grouping}{sknFac10}
   
Factorising by grouping is a technique where we group terms in a way that allows us to factorise
 common factors from each group. This method is especially useful when dealing with
 four-term expressions.

\tcbline

Factorise $2am+2an-bm-bn$\\ 
    \begin{mtask}
       \task[] Step \circled{\ncnt} Group the terms\\
               \-\hspace{2cm} Start by grouping the terms into two pairs: 
			   \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.3cm] \item[] \boxNote[bisAnswer]{$(2am+2an)-(bm+bn) $}  \end{itemize}

       \task[]  Step \circled{\ncnt} Factorise the common factors from each group.
	            \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.3cm] \item[] \boxNote[bisAnswer]{$2a(m+n)-b(m+n) $}  \end{itemize} 

       \task[]  Step \circled{\ncnt} Factorise the common binomial factor\\
               \-\hspace{2cm} Both terms share the common binomial factor $(m+n)$. So, we factorise out $(m+n)$:
               \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.3cm] \item[] \boxNote[bisAnswer]{$2a(m+n)-b(m+n)=(2a-b)(m+n) $} \end{itemize}
\end{mtask}

\tcbline
Solution:\\
      \-\hspace{2cm}$\begin{sArrow}
			             2am+2an-bm-bn 
                       & = (2am+2an)-(bm+bn)   \\
                       & = 2a(m+n)-b(m+n)      \\
					   & = (2a-b)(m+n)
	                \end{sArrow}$
\tcbline  
Key Concept:
\begin{mtask}
    \task[\HandRight] \uwave{Factorising by grouping} involves \uwave{grouping the terms into pairs} that share common factors.
    \task[\HandRight] By factorising the common terms from each pair, we make the expression simpler and easier to factorise further, if necessary.
\end{mtask}
\end{boxSkill}
